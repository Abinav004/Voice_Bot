# Parking and getting here

The clinic has 14 parking spaces in the basement of the building, reached from
the 4th Block service lane rather than the main road. Parking is free for
patients for up to two hours; validate your ticket at reception before you
leave. The basement has a height limit of 2.1 metres, so larger vehicles should
use the paid public lot behind the Jyoti Nivas College bus stop, about a
four-minute walk away.

Two-wheelers park at ground level beside the ramp at no charge. There is no
valet service.

The nearest metro station is Koramangala on the Pink Line, about a twelve-minute
walk. Buses 201R and 500D stop directly outside the building.

# Wheelchair access and mobility

The building entrance from the service lane is step-free and there is a lift to
all floors. The consulting rooms and the waiting area are all on the same level.
There is an accessible toilet next to the waiting area.

If a patient needs help getting from the car park to reception, call ahead and a
staff member will meet them at the basement lift. A wheelchair is kept at
reception and can be borrowed during the visit at no charge.

# Insurance and reimbursement

The clinic does not bill insurers directly, and there is no cashless facility.
Payment is taken in full at the time of the visit, and reception issues a
detailed receipt and a signed consultation summary that most insurers accept for
reimbursement.

If a patient's insurer requires a specific claim form, they should bring it with
them and hand it to reception before the consultation, not afterwards, as the
doctor needs to complete the clinical section during the visit. Completed forms
are usually ready within two working days and can be collected from reception or
sent by email.

Corporate health plans through employers work the same way — pay at the visit,
claim afterwards. The clinic cannot tell a patient in advance how much their
insurer will reimburse.

# Prescription refills

Repeat prescriptions for an existing, ongoing medication can be issued without a
new consultation if the patient has been seen at the clinic for that condition
within the last six months. Request a refill by calling reception; it is
normally ready for collection the next working day.

If the last visit for that condition was more than six months ago, a follow-up
consultation is required before the prescription can be reissued. This applies
regardless of how long the patient has been taking the medication.

Refills cannot be issued over the phone for any controlled medication, sedative
or sleeping tablet. These always need an in-person consultation.

The clinic does not dispense medicines. Prescriptions are taken to any pharmacy.

# What to bring to a first visit

New patients should arrive ten minutes early to register. Bring a government
photo ID, any previous prescriptions or discharge summaries, a list of current
medications including anything bought over the counter, and any recent test
results even if they were done elsewhere.

For a child's appointment, bring the immunisation card. For a dermatology
appointment, avoid wearing makeup or nail polish on the area being examined, as
it can hide what the doctor needs to see.

# Test results from outside labs

The clinic has no laboratory of its own, so blood tests and scans are done at
external centres. Patients are given a requisition to take to a lab of their
choice.

Results sent directly to the clinic by a lab are reviewed within two working
days. Reception will call if the doctor wants to discuss them or bring the
patient in sooner. No news within three working days means nothing urgent was
found, but patients are welcome to call and check.

Results are never given out over the phone by reception staff, and never to
anyone other than the patient themselves or a parent in the case of a child.

# Waiting times and delays

Appointments run to schedule most of the time, but a doctor dealing with an
urgent case can put the clinic thirty to forty minutes behind. Reception will
call if a delay of more than twenty minutes is expected.

Patients who arrive more than fifteen minutes late may be asked to wait until
there is a gap, or to rebook, since the slot cannot be extended into the next
patient's time.

# Repeat and follow-up visits

A follow-up within fifteen days of the last visit for the same problem is
charged at the reduced follow-up rate. After fifteen days it is a full
consultation again, even for the same condition.

Follow-ups are with the same doctor wherever possible. If that doctor is not in
on a day the patient can attend, reception will say so rather than book them
with someone unfamiliar with the case.
